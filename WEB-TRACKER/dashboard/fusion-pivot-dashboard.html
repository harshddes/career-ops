The Reality Check

Can you break into fusion with zero direct experience?

YES — and it's been done before. Joy Dunn spent a decade in aerospace before becoming VP Manufacturing at CFS. Alex Creely went from Mechanical Engineering undergrad to CFS employee #13. David Kirtley got his PhD in Aerospace at Michigan and now runs Helion — a $1B+ fusion company. CFS's diagnostic engineer posting explicitly encourages aerospace backgrounds.

But let's be honest about the gaps

Your plasma diagnostics background is a genuine asset. But you lack: (1) tokamak/stellarator-specific operational experience, (2) nuclear physics depth (MCNP, neutronics), (3) years of post-degree tenure that senior roles demand. The play is entry-level diagnostics or internship-to-hire pathways, not senior scientist roles.

Your transferable skill overlap

Higher than you think. CEM/ESA calibration = charged particle detector characterization. SIMION = ion-optics beam transport. LVACCS test rig = plasma source commissioning. FPGA readout = real-time detector signal processing. The physics is the same physics. The plasmas are just hotter and more expensive.

The best path forward

Two parallel tracks: (1) Apply now to career-changer-friendly companies (CFS, Helion, Pacific Fusion internship). (2) Simultaneously prepare PhD applications for Fall 2027 at MIT PSFC, Princeton, Max Planck IPP, or EPFL — all fully funded. If industry works, great. If not, you'll have world-class academic options ready.
